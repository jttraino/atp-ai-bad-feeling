# Speaker Instructions — ATP Event, Sept 17, 2026

## 1. Your machine, your responsibility

You are responsible for having your laptop fully configured before the mandatory test (below) — Microsoft Teams desktop app installed, updated, and signed in. Don't wait for event day to discover an install/update issue.

## 2. Mandatory dry run — at least one week before the event

The organizer will create the actual meeting you'll use on the day (a Teams channel meeting) and send you the link at least a week out. You must join that test meeting and run through the full flow: start recording, start transcription, talk for a minute, check that captions/transcript are being generated, then end the meeting properly. If it works in the test, it'll work identically on the day — same link, same flow, no surprises. If something's wrong, this is the time to fix it, not Sept 17.

## 3. Microphone

Built-in laptop mic is fine *if tested*. If you have a personal mic you already know and trust — a lapel/wireless mic like a Hollyland Lark, DJI Mic, or similar — feel free to use it instead, as long as it doesn't get in the way of your talk (cabling, range, having to stay near the laptop, etc.). Whatever you use, it must be the exact setup you test in the dry run — don't switch mics between the test and the real event.

## 4. Day-of flow

- Join your Teams meeting a few minutes early, start recording + transcription *before* you begin talking.
- Open with your name, station, and topic spoken out loud — this becomes a marker in the transcript.
- **Restate every audience question out loud before you answer it** — the mic likely won't pick up the room.
- Close with a spoken line ("that's the end of this session"), then properly end the meeting (don't just close the laptop) so the transcript finalizes correctly.

## 5. Backup recording

Also run a simple voice memo app on your phone during the talk, independent of Teams. It's just insurance — if anything on the Teams/laptop side fails, we still have audio.

## 6. Content, ahead of time

Please send your talk outline/notes (as close to a script as you have) plus a 2-3 sentence abstract before the event. This becomes the fallback if a recording has issues, and helps prep the closing keynote framework.
