# Speaker Instructions, ATP Event, September 17, 2026 ABY

## What your session actually is

Your base is a guided discussion, not a talk. You work from a list of roughly eight questions that you put to your base, and the room answers. You won't get to all of them, and that's fine. Your job is to keep the discussion moving and to make sure it lands on the recording.

## Before the Event

### 1. Your dry run with Fleet Command

"Your eyes can deceive you. Don't trust them." Obi-Wan didn't let Luke fly blind without a rehearsal first. Neither should you. John Trainor is Fleet Command for this event. He's hosting and recording every base's Teams meeting.

**Your dry run gets booked live during the prep meeting on Monday, September 14.** Bring your calendar. Everyone walks out of that meeting with a 15-minute slot on the books, held between the 14th and the 16th. Don't wait to be chased.

The dry run uses your actual meeting link and the exact setup you'll use on the day. You'll confirm you get in with no lobby or waiting room, confirm your audio, confirm noise suppression is off, and confirm recording and transcription are capturing correctly. You'll also walk a few steps away from the laptop and talk like an attendee would, because that's the audio that actually matters. If it works in the test, it works on the day. If anything's off, this is when to fix it.

### 2. Your setup

- Install the Teams desktop app and sign in ahead of the dry run.
- **Your laptop's built-in mic, and nothing else.** No lapel mics, no wireless mics, no phone acting as a second microphone. The format is you putting questions to a room and the room answering, and every extra audio source adds one more thing to manage and one more way to lose the session. One laptop, one audio source.
- **Turn Teams noise suppression off.** Not Auto, off. Settings > Devices > Noise suppression > Off. This is the single most important setting on your machine. Noise suppression is built to isolate one person at a desk, which means it will strip out exactly the room discussion we're there to capture. Set it during the dry run and re-check it on the day.
- Position the laptop for the room, not for you. It doesn't need to present anything, so put it wherever it has the best chance of hearing the discussion.
- **Bring your own laptop, plugged into power.** Yours, specifically, so you know the password and can change your own settings. Then: keep it plugged in, don't let it sleep, and don't let it lock. A machine that goes to sleep stops the meeting, and a locked machine that nobody can get back into is the most likely way a base loses its transcript entirely.
- You don't need to be Luke sniping womp rats in a T-16 back home. But you do need to stay about that close to your mic: "not much bigger than two meters." (Well, maybe just one meter even.)

### 3. Your questions

Tom Lasswell is drafting the question list for each base. Once you have it, it's yours: read it, cut anything you wouldn't actually ask, reword what you want, and send the result back. A few ground rules that make the whole event work:

- **Roughly eight questions**, sized to what you can genuinely get through. Not a long menu you'll only scratch. If the list is longer than your session, cut it.
- **Get your final version to John no later than Tuesday, September 15.** Wednesday is workable, Thursday morning is the outer edge. Late is survivable. Missing is not.
- **Don't radically rewrite the list at the last minute.** Trimming a question or rewording one is fine, any time. Swapping the whole set on the day is not.
- **Stay reasonably close to your list in the room.** Following the discussion where it goes is good facilitation, and it's fine as long as the audio is clean. The catch is that nobody knows whether the audio was clean until after the session is over, so the questions are the one thing we can count on.

That last point is the real reason this matters. Your question list, with likely answers pre-filled against it, is the backup plan. If your recording or transcript has any problem on the day, that list is what represents your base in the closing keynote. The more accurate it is, the better your base comes through even in a worst case. I'd rather not have to send IG-88 after you to track it down.

## Day of the Event

- **Arrive at 5:15 PM.** Earlier than you'd think necessary, for two reasons: Roswell traffic on a Thursday evening, and the fact that we want your laptop set up, its settings confirmed, and a live test meeting run on the actual venue Wi-Fi before the room fills up.
- Join your Teams meeting a few minutes early. Think of it like the fleet waiting on the shield to come down before the attack can begin. You want it down in time, not a moment late.
- Open with your name, base, and topic spoken out loud. This becomes a marker in the transcript.
- **Restate every audience question out loud before you answer it.** The mic likely won't pick up the room.
- **You'll have an Astromech.** Every base gets one: a volunteer whose only job is to watch the machine so you don't have to. They confirm recording is running, that the live transcript is actually building, and that nothing has gone to sleep, locked, or dropped off the network. If you brought your own backup recorder, they also carry it around the room (see below). R2 rode in the socket watching the systems so the pilot could fly. Same idea. Tell them at the start that power, sleep, and lock are what you want watched, and then go run your room.
- When you're done, **explicitly end the meeting.** Don't just close your laptop, hit Leave, or walk away. Ending the meeting is what tells Teams to finalize the session and start generating the transcript. Leaving it does not.

### Backup recording, encouraged if you already have a setup

If you already run your own recorder or transcription service and trust it, please use it. A second independent capture of your base is genuinely valuable, and it's the difference between a lost transcript being an inconvenience and being a hole in the keynote. Two conditions:

- **You need to be able to get it to John quickly and reliably.** Email it to **john@johntrainor.com as soon as your session ends**, not later that evening and not the next morning. If your service holds the file behind a login, a manual export step, or a processing queue you can't predict, it isn't usable as backup and you're better off skipping it.
- **Keep it genuinely separate from Teams.** If it runs on the same computer, test that exact combination during your dry run, because the two fighting over microphone access is worse than having no backup at all. A separate phone or recorder avoids the problem entirely.

Here's the part worth planning for: **your Astromech can carry it around the room.** The laptop is fixed in place and hearing the room at a disadvantage. A handheld recorder walking toward whoever's talking hears what the laptop can't. That's the one thing a second device does better than the primary capture, so if you're bringing one, hand it to your Astromech and let them work the room with it.
