# Speaker Instructions, ATP Event, September 17, 2026 ABY

## Before the Event

### 1. Schedule your dry run with Fleet Command

"Your eyes can deceive you. Don't trust them." Obi-Wan didn't let Luke fly blind without a rehearsal first. Neither should you. John Trainor is Fleet Command for this event. He's hosting and recording every base's Teams meeting. Reach out to him to get a test of your actual meeting link scheduled, ideally at least a week before the event. You'll join together, confirm you get in with no lobby or waiting room, test your audio, and confirm recording and transcription are capturing correctly. It's the same link and setup you'll use on September 17. If it works in the test, it works on the day. If anything's off, this is when to fix it.

### 2. Your setup

- Install the Teams desktop app and sign in ahead of the dry run.
- Built-in laptop mic is fine if tested, just like your computer will be during the presentation.
- If you have a personal mic you already know and trust, a lapel or wireless mic like a Hollyland Lark or a DJI Mic, feel free to use it instead. Just make sure it doesn't get in the way of your talk. Keep it identical between the dry run and the real event.
- Plug your laptop into power during the presentation. If it goes to sleep, the meeting stops.
- You don't need to be Luke sniping womp rats in a T-16 back home. But you do need to stay about that close to your mic: "not much bigger than two meters." (Well, maybe just one meter even.)

### 3. Send your talk notes, as much detail as possible

Send an outline of what you're planning to present. The more complete, the better. A near-script is ideal, even a bullet list helps. This isn't just background for us. It's the backup plan if your recording or transcript has any issue on the day, so the more it captures, the better your base still comes through in the closing keynote, even in a worst case.

**Deadline: no later than the day before the event.** I don't want to have to send IG-88 after you to track it down. But don't wait until then if you don't have to. Send what you have as soon as you have it, even as a rough draft, and send an update later if your talk changes.

## Day of the Event

- Join your Teams meeting a few minutes early. Think of it like the fleet waiting on the shield to come down before the attack can begin. You want it down in time, not a moment late.
- Open with your name, base, and topic spoken out loud. This becomes a marker in the transcript.
- **Restate every audience question out loud before you answer it.** The mic likely won't pick up the room.
- When you're done, **explicitly end the meeting.** Don't just close your laptop or walk away. This is the clear signal that tells Teams to finalize and prepare the transcript.

### Backup recording

If you already have a recording setup you're used to running, a phone voice memo app or anything else, feel free to run it during your talk as backup, independent of Teams. If that backup runs on your computer alongside Teams, make sure the two don't conflict (microphone access, especially) and test that exact combination ahead of time in your dry run. Then just send the file to John afterward. It's insurance: if anything on the Teams side fails, we still have audio.

## After the Event

- Nothing further required on your end. Once you've ended the meeting per above, you're done.
- If you ran your own backup recording, send it to John.
- Your base's transcript will be posted publicly in this repo as part of the event's open archive. If there's anything you'd want reviewed before it goes up, flag it with John before the event.

The Force will be with you, always.
